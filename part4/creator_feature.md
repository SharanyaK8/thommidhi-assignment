Part 4: Creators, creatively
The creator

I chose Sowjanya’s Smart Kitchen, an Indian cooking channel that posts simple homemade recipes using ingredients easily available at home. On the channel page I checked on 14 October 2026, it showed 46.1K subscribers and 1,095 videos, and it joined YouTube on 2 September 2020. By my own calculation, that is roughly three videos a week on average. I did not check how many are Shorts.

On the same day I watched the video “Day 5 & 6 Dusshera Navaratri Special Naivedyam Recipes || Sweet Pongal & Daddyojanam || Specials”. The creator does not speak in it. There is only music, and the recipe appears as English text on the screen, for example “moong dal, 1/4 cup, dry roast on low flame till golden brown.” The channel description is also written only in English.

I also saw that some titles and thumbnails use Telugu, for example “Beet Root Halwa Recipe (బీట్ రూట్ హల్వా) || Simple, Easy, Tasty & Healthy || Simple Sweet Recipes”. This suggests the creator wants to reach Telugu-speaking viewers, but the steps and quantities inside the video are only in English. A viewer can be invited in Telugu and then still have to read English to cook.

I checked three videos. The comments I saw were few, and they were short English praise like “fantastic”. I did not see anyone asking for quantities or for another language, so comments gave me no direct evidence of what viewers need. I did not check the subtitle languages, so I don’t know if other languages are already available.

The feature: a recipe translation helper

The creator already writes the recipe in English for the screen. The helper takes that text and translates it into a language the creator chooses. The translated text can go into the video description and a pinned comment. If the creator wants, it can also make a short voice-over script for people who prefer listening.

I think this would save time every week, because translating a full recipe by hand and checking it takes a long time, and this creator posts often. So I would not translate every video. The creator would pick the videos that matter most, like festival specials, and I would start with only one language. Since the titles and thumbnails already use Telugu, Telugu would be my first choice, and I would add a second language only if the first works well. The creator reads and corrects everything before it is used, and nothing is posted automatically.

What could go wrong, and how I would protect them

The biggest risk is a wrong number. If “1/4 cup” becomes “1/4 tablespoon,” the dish is spoiled, and a wrong ingredient could even be dangerous for someone with an allergy. So I would not let the model change numbers and units freely. A small program would check that every number and unit in the translation matches the English line and flag any difference. This check would catch some mistakes, but it would not guarantee that the whole translation is correct, so the creator would still need to review it before publishing. This is the same idea as my Part 3 answer: numbers should not be invented by the model.

The second risk is that the translation sounds unnatural, or that a dish or ingredient has a different name in another region. I would ask a native Telugu speaker to check the first few translations, and the creator should not publish a language that nobody trustworthy has checked.

The third risk is the voice. An AI voice may pronounce dish names wrongly, so it should be optional, labelled as an AI voice, and heard by the creator before use.

How I would know after one month that it helped

The videos I looked at had few comments, mostly short praise, so I would not use comments to measure success. Before starting, I would ask the creator how much time translation or subtitles take in a normal week. After four weeks, I would ask again.

I would also count how many translated lines the creator had to correct and how many the number check flagged. Many errors would mean the helper is not good enough yet. I would look at the creator’s YouTube analytics to see whether they give useful information about viewer language or region. I would be careful about saying the helper caused any change, because views can also change due to festivals, topics, and other factors. Finally, I would check whether the creator is still using the helper in week four. If they stopped, that tells me more than any survey.
